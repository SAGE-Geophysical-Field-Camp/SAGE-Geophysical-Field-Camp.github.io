**The Goal**

We would like all SAGE students to arrive in New Mexico with a basic footing in the geological and volcanological topics for understanding our 2026 science plan as well as a solid set of ideas regarding the physical basis for geophysical techniques we will be applying in the field. The application of all our geophysical techniques to solve problems is based on a solid understanding of the physical properties of rocks, and that’s where we will start our in-person SAGE. Therefore, we would like you to do some preparation and pre-thinking about rock types and their physical properties.

 

**The Activities**

We will help you reach these goals by facilitating the following activities in a workshop-style format during our Virtual SAGE meetings. **The workshops will be very participatory** so expect discussion, rather than lecture. We want to help you construct your own knowledge base, which will help you better remember what you learn. Here are the types of activities we will complete:

* Readings

**The Format**

All of the Virtual SAGE workshop sessions will have a similar format:

**SAGE Guidebook Summary Guidance**

This assignment will be in two parts.

1\. SAGE Guidebook Summary

The final summary is different from the 3-5 sentences that you are writing for each of the week’s topics—the weekly summaries are short, and everyone will do them for each topic. For the SAGE Guidebook, you have been assigned 1 topic and we ask that you bring together what was contributed by you and your fellow participants during our workshop to write some introductory material for the guidebook.

Here is some additional guidance:

* **Content:** The content for these summaries should be accessible for folks newly trying to learn the science. **Consider your audience to be a future SAGE participant, who maybe never had a chance to learn about your topic in school.** What would you want them to know before they arrive at SAGE?  Keep the focus on the physical (or chemical) basics for the topic, and then use the three questions we are discussing in the workshops as a guide: 1\) Why is this topic important for understanding a caldera using geophysics? 2\) What is the physical property measured by the geophysical method(s) and the associated units? 3\) What type(s) of rocks in the subsurface would show a good contrast for this property? If your topic is more geological than geophysical, think about explaining why it is important to understand this topic for the interpretation of geophysical data. No need to get into any details about instrumentation, data processing or interpretation—we will be covering those topics at in-person SAGE.  
* **Resources to use:** You may use anything you want\!  Of course, you have the readings and videos, notes from our workshop sessions, and your fellow students’ short summaries. We are hoping that will suffice and you do not need to spend a ton of time digging about in other resources to find additional information. But feel free to do so if you wish\!  
* **A Note on AI:** These writing assignments are designed to leverage writing to help you learn. Past SAGE Participants have said that doing the writing summaries was a valuable part of their learning process during SAGE, therefore we would like you to have that experience too. Using AI to do the writing will greatly decrease what you learn and retain from these assignments. Using AI to find resources on the web that more closely match what you are looking for may be a good use, but please engage in your own writing process to summarize your findings from the original sources.   
* **Images?** If you think an image captures a point you want to make better than words, please do add images/diagrams/etc. to your text\!  Make sure it has some words along with it describing how it supports or illustrates a certain point.  
* **How much to write:** There are not hard goals or limits on length but generally ½-1 page is about right. Think about when you go to a web resource and you are trying to understand if some topic will be useful for you to learn. What length would you want to see on a web page you visit, and aim for that\!  More than one page is likely too long.
